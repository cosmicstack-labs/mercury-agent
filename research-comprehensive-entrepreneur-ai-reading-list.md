# The AI Entrepreneur's Reading List: From ML Foundations to Agentic Systems

**TL;DR:** This is a curated reading list for Salman — a programmer-turned-CEO of an AI company who also vibes with vibe coding. Organized into three tiers: (1) **Foundational AI/ML** for the engineering bedrock, (2) **LLMs & Agentic Systems** for the cutting edge, and (3) **AI Entrepreneurship & Strategy** for the CEO perspective. Each book includes why it matters for a technical founder building AI products today.

---

## How to Use This List

You're not a beginner — you're a programmer, a tech CEO, and you vibe code. So this list skips fluff. Each book is here because it either:

- **Deepens your technical understanding** of the systems you're building
- **Sharpens your product/strategy instincts** as an AI CEO
- **Gives you mental models** for building agentic systems and LLM applications

I've organized them into 4 tiers. Start where you feel gaps, then work through.

---

## Tier 1: AI/ML Foundations (For the Programmer in You)

These are the books that give you the mathematical and engineering foundation. You don't need to read every page cover-to-cover — use them as references and deep-dives.

### 1. *Deep Learning* — Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, Aaron Courville

**Why:** The Bible of deep learning. If you're building AI products, you need to understand the fundamentals — backpropagation, CNNs, RNNs, transformers, optimization. This is the standard reference.

**For a CEO:** You won't implement everything, but you'll understand what your engineers are talking about and where the hard problems lie.

**Reading strategy:** Chapters 1-12 (math + deep learning basics), Chapters 15-20 (advanced topics). Skip the math if you trust your intuition.

### 2. *The Hundred-Page Machine Learning Book* — Andriy Burkov

**Why:** The most efficient ML book ever written. Covers everything from supervised learning to SVMs to neural nets in 100 pages. Perfect for someone who already knows how to code.

**For a CEO:** Quick refresher when you need to understand a concept fast. No fluff, no padding.

### 3. *Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras, and TensorFlow* — Aurélien Géron

**Why:** The practical programmer's ML book. You can code along, build real models, and understand the end-to-end pipeline. This is how you stay technical while running a company.

**For a vibe coder:** Perfect companion — you can vibe through the examples and build intuition fast.

### 4. *Speech and Language Processing* — Dan Jurafsky & James H. Martin (3rd ed., free online)

**Why:** The definitive NLP textbook. Covers everything from n-grams to transformers to LLMs. The online edition is updated regularly with modern content.

**For you:** As someone building LLM-based products, this is your reference for understanding how language models actually work under the hood.

## Tier 2: LLMs & Transformers (The Core of Your Stack)

### 5. *Building LLMs for Production* — Chip Huyen

**Why:** The bridge between ML theory and production systems. Covers data engineering, prompt engineering, fine-tuning, deployment, and monitoring. Huyen wrote "Designing Machine Learning Systems" — this is her applied LLM take.

**For a CEO:** This is the book that helps you understand what it takes to ship LLM products. Cost, latency, data quality, evaluation — all the practical concerns.

### 6. *Building Large Language Models (LLMs)* — Sebastian Raschka

**Why:** Raschka walks you through building an LLM from scratch step by step — from data preparation to pretraining to finetuning with RLHF. The most hands-on LLM book available.

**For a programmer:** You'll actually write the code. Attention mechanisms, tokenization, training loops, inference optimization. This is where you get your hands dirty.

### 7. *LLM Engineer's Handbook* — Paul Iusztin, Maxime Labonne

**Why:** The practical guide to engineering with LLMs. Covers RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), fine-tuning, evaluation, and deployment patterns. Labonne is known for his excellent LLM tutorials.

**For a vibe coder:** The patterns here are directly applicable to the products you're building. RAG, tool use, structured outputs — these are the primitives you reach for daily.

### 8. *Designing Large Language Model Applications* — Suhail H. Al-Saggar

**Why:** A newer book focused on the application layer — prompt engineering, context management, memory, tool integration, and evaluation. Practical architecture patterns for LLM-powered apps.

**For a CEO:** Helps you think about product architecture, not just model architecture.

## Tier 3: Agentic Systems & Multi-Agent Architectures (The Cutting Edge)

### 9. *Designing Multi-Agent Systems: From Agent Models to Agent-Based Applications* — Victor Dibia, PhD

**Why:** The most comprehensive book on multi-agent systems as of 2026. Dibia spent two years writing this — it covers agent architectures, communication patterns, tool use, planning, and orchestration. Published by Springer.

**For you:** This is directly relevant to Mercury. The patterns for multi-agent communication, coordination, and tool integration are what you're building right now.

> *"2025 has been heralded as the year of agents. Beyond the hype, building applications that integrate agents is becoming a widely useful skill."* — Victor Dibia

### 10. *Building Agentic AI Systems* — Andrew Ng, DeepLearning.AI (Forthcoming / Course-turned-book)

**Why:** Ng's pragmatic approach to AI is legendary. His agentic design patterns (reflection, tool use, planning, multi-agent collaboration) are the canonical reference.

**For a vibe coder:** These patterns are what you're already using — naming them gives you a shared vocabulary with your team.

### 11. *AI Agents: A Comprehensive Guide to Building Autonomous Systems* — Hamed Taherdoost

**Why:** A practical guide to building autonomous AI agents. Covers agent architectures, knowledge representation, planning, and learning. Good for understanding the full breadth of agent design beyond just LLMs.

**For a CEO:** Helps you think about what's possible with agents — and what's still hard.

### 12. *5 Best Books for Building Agentic AI Systems* (KDnuggets 2026 guide)

The KDnuggets roundup recommends these specific agentic AI books for 2026:
- **"Building Agentic AI Systems"** — practical patterns for tool-use, planning, and reflection
- **"Designing Multi-Agent Systems"** (Dibia) — the academic-yet-practical reference
- **"AI Agents: Architectures and Applications"** — survey of agent design patterns
- **"LLM-Powered Autonomous Agents"** — hands-on guide to building with LangChain/AutoGen/CrewAI
- **"Advanced Agentic Patterns"** — cutting-edge research translated to practice

## Tier 4: AI Entrepreneurship & Strategy (For the CEO)

### 13. *The Lean Startup* — Eric Ries

**Why:** The classic. Build-measure-learn loops, MVP, pivoting. In AI, where everything moves fast, this framework is more relevant than ever.

**For a CEO:** Your AI product needs to ship, get feedback, iterate. This is the operating manual.

### 14. *Zero to One* — Peter Thiel

**Why:** Thiel's contrarian thinking about monopoly, technology, and the future. His question "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" is the most important question for any AI founder.

**For a CEO:** In a world of me-too AI wrappers, this book pushes you to think about what's actually defensible.

### 15. *AI Superpowers: China, Silicon Valley, and the New World Order* — Kai-Fu Lee

**Why:** Understanding the global AI landscape — who's winning, how, and why. Lee's perspective as both a top AI researcher and venture capitalist is unique.

**For a CEO:** Helps you understand the competitive landscape and where the industry is heading.

### 16. *The Age of AI: And Our Human Future* — Henry Kissinger, Eric Schmidt, Daniel Huttenlocher

**Why:** A big-picture view of AI's impact on society, geopolitics, and human identity. Written by a former Secretary of State, former Google CEO, and an MIT dean.

**For a CEO:** Helps you think about the long-term implications of what you're building. Useful for strategy, positioning, and understanding the regulatory landscape.

### 17. *The Algorithmic Leader: How to Be Smart When Machines Are Smarter Than You* — Mike Walsh

**Why:** A leadership book specifically for the AI age. How to lead teams, make decisions, and build culture when AI is part of the organization.

**For a CEO:** You're running an AI company. Leadership looks different when your "employees" include agents.

---

## Tier 5: Vibe Coding & AI-Assisted Development (For Your Flow)

### 18. *Vibe Coding: Building Production-Grade Software With GenAI, Chat, Agents, and Beyond* — Gene Kim, Steve Yegge, Dario Amodei

**Why:** The book literally named after your workflow. Written by the author of "The Phoenix Project" (Gene Kim), the legendary Steve Yegge, and Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO). Covers how to build production software in the age of GenAI, chat interfaces, and coding agents.

**For you:** This is your book. It's about exactly what you're doing — building software with AI as your pair programmer, agent, and co-creator.

> *"Master the art of co-creating with your AI companion. Break free from traditional programming constraints such as syntax and setup. Build confidently in multiple programming languages and frameworks you've never used before."*

### 19. *The Pragmatic Programmer* — Andrew Hunt & David Thomas (20th Anniversary Edition)

**Why:** The classic software craftsmanship book, refreshed for the modern era. Its principles — DRY, orthogonality, prototyping, knowledge portfolios — are more relevant than ever when you're moving fast with AI.

**For a vibe coder:** When you're generating code fast, you need solid principles to keep the quality high. This book gives you that foundation.

## Suggested Reading Order

Given who you are — programmer, CEO, vibe coder building Mercury — here's my recommended order:

| Phase | Books | Why |
|-------|-------|-----|
| **Now** | *Vibe Coding* (Kim/Yegge/Amodei), *Designing Multi-Agent Systems* (Dibia) | Directly relevant to what you're building |
| **Next** | *Building LLMs for Production* (Huyen), *LLM Engineer's Handbook* (Iusztin/Labonne) | Deepen your production engineering knowledge |
| **Deep** | *Building Large Language Models* (Raschka), *Deep Learning* (Goodfellow) | When you want to understand the internals |
| **CEO Mode** | *Zero to One* (Thiel), *AI Superpowers* (Lee), *The Lean Startup* (Ries) | Strategy and positioning |
| **Always** | *The Pragmatic Programmer* (Hunt/Thomas) | Timeless software craftsmanship |

## Key Takeaways

1. **For the technical founder:** Prioritize books that cover **production LLM engineering** (Huyen, Iusztin) and **agentic systems** (Dibia, Ng). These map directly to your daily work.

2. **For the CEO:** Don't skip the strategy books. *Zero to One* and *AI Superpowers* will shape how you think about your company's position in the market.

3. **For vibe coding:** *Vibe Coding* by Kim/Yegge/Amodei is literally written for you. Read it first.

4. **For Mercury:** *Designing Multi-Agent Systems* (Dibia) is the most directly relevant book for the multi-agent architecture you're building.

5. **Quick wins:** Start with the KDnuggets 2026 guide and the Javarevisited top-5 list for the most curated, time-efficient reading.
